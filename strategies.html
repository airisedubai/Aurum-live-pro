Options Basics for Beginners

What are options? Think of an option like a "coupon" or "ticket" that gives you the right to buy or sell something at a specific price, at a specific time.

Call Option - You buy the RIGHT to purchase gold at price X (like a coupon to buy gold even if price goes up). You profit when price goes UP.

Put Option - You buy the RIGHT to sell gold at price X (like a coupon to sell gold even if price goes down). You profit when price goes DOWN.

Premium - The price you pay to buy the option (like buying a ticket). This is your maximum loss.

Strike Price - The price X in the option (the price you have the right to buy/sell at).

Expiry - When the option ticket expires (you must use it or lose it).

Example: Gold is at $4,096. You buy a CALL option for gold at $4,100 strike, paying a $20 premium. If gold goes to $4,200 at expiry, your option is worth $100. You keep $100 - $20 premium = $80 profit (4x return!). If gold stays below $4,100, the option expires worthless - you lose the $20 premium only.

Key Risk: Options can expire worthless. Only risk money you can afford to lose. The maximum loss is the premium paid.

Why use options? Leverage - control $4,000+ of gold with only $20 premium. Limited risk - worst case you lose the premium only.

🧸 Strategy for Laymen (Simple English)

No jargon. No complex terms. Just simple explanations anyone can understand.

1. The "Rubber Band" Trick

(Mean Reversion Strategy)

Imagine a rubber band. If you stretch it too far up, it snap backs down. If you pull it too far down, it snap backs up.

  • Price goes way up too fast? Don't buy! Wait for snap back (Sell).
  • Price crashes way down? Don't panic sell! Wait for snap back (Buy).
  • Rule: Never chase price when rubber band is stretched tight.

2. The "River Flow"

(Trend Following Strategy)

Imagine you're in a boat on a river. It's much easier to swim with the current than against it.

  • Uptrend (river flowing up): Only look for Buy. Ignore small dips.
  • Downtrend (river flowing down): Only look for Sell. Ignore small bounces.
  • Rule: "The Trend is your Friend." Don't try to stop the river.

3. The "Floor and Ceiling"

(Support & Resistance)

Imagine a ball bouncing in a room. It hits the Floor and bounces up. It hits the Ceiling and falls down.

  • Floor (Support): Price level where buyers wait. Good place to Buy.
  • Ceiling (Resistance): Price level where sellers wait. Good place to Sell.
  • Breakout: If ball smashes through ceiling, ceiling becomes new floor!

4. The "Traffic Light"

(Risk Management)

Trading without rules is like driving without traffic lights. You will crash.

  • 🔴 Red Light (Stop Loss): Max money you'll lose. If hit, MUST exit. No hoping.
  • 🟢 Green Light (Take Profit): Your goal. Hit it, take money, celebrate.
  • 🟡 Yellow Light (Caution): Big news (CPI/NFP). Stay out until it passes.

📖 Key Concepts for Gold Trading

Why Gold Moves

  • USD Strength — Gold and DXY are inversely correlated. Weak dollar → gold up.
  • Real Yields — When US 10Y TIPS yield drops, gold rises (lower opportunity cost of holding non-yielding asset).
  • Fed Policy — Rate cuts → gold up. Rate hikes → gold down.
  • Geopolitics — Wars, conflicts, uncertainty → gold safe-haven bid.
  • Inflation — Gold is an inflation hedge. Rising CPI → gold up.
  • Central Bank Buying — China, India, Russia accumulating gold → bullish structural trend.

Gold Scalping Tips

  • Use 5m or 1m charts for entry timing
  • Set SL at 1.5× ATR from entry (dynamic, not fixed pips)
  • Target TP1 at 1× R:R, TP2 at 2× R:R — bank TP1+TP2, let TP3 ride
  • Trade only during London + New York overlap (11:30–14:30 GMT) for max liquidity
  • Check DXY and real yields for directional bias before entering
  • Use the Aurum Engine checklist for disciplined entries — never skip the setup

Risk Management Rules

  • Never risk more than 0.5–1% of equity per trade
  • Maximum 3 concurrent positions
  • Stop loss is never widened — if hit, exit and reassess
  • Take profits at TP1 + TP2 first, then move SL to breakeven
  • No trading during high-impact news (NFP, FOMC, CPI) unless you have a specific strategy for it
